# Chapter 3 — The First Subject

The wind in the Wastes did not blow; it pushed. It was a heavy, stagnant pressure that tasted of cold ash and forgotten names. Behind Egris, the two gates were now nothing more than thin silver needles on the horizon. Ahead, there was only the Gray—a jagged landscape of broken stone and swirling mist where the laws of the living had no weight.

He had walked for what felt like hours, his boots crunching on the calcified remains of structures that had no geometry.

"You are going the wrong way, Great Egris."

The voice was thin, like parchment tearing.

Egris didn't stop, but his hand instinctively drifted to the empty space at his hip where his saber should have been. He turned his head slightly.

A man was following him, keeping a distance of exactly ten paces. He was thin, stooped, and wore the tattered silk of a court official. His fingers were stained permanently black with ink, and a heavy wooden satchel was slung across his chest, rattling with every step.

"There is no 'way' here," Egris said. "Therefore, there is no wrong one."

"There is the way of the many, and the way of the fool," the scribe replied, struggling to keep pace. "The many stay near the gates, hoping for a stray crumb of grace or a familiar face. The fools walk into the Wastes to be eaten by the Hollow. You appear to be the latter."

Egris stopped and turned fully. The scribe flinched, but he did not run. He looked up at Egris through spectacles that were cracked, his eyes wide and trembling.

"You knew me," Egris observed.

"I spent twenty-two years in a stone room in Samarkand recording your tax decrees and your execution lists," the scribe said, a bitter edge to his voice. "I know the shape of your signature better than I know the face of my own mother. My name was Arghun. I was a scribe of the empire you burned."

"And now?"

"Now I am a dead man in a place where even stories die," Arghun said, clutching his satchel. "The Wastes eat memory, Great Egris. Every hour you spend out here, a piece of who you were flakes away. Your first victory, the name of your first horse, the color of your wife's eyes... the Gray takes it all until you are just another shadow screaming at the wind."

"If you know this, why are you following me into the dark?"

Arghun looked out at the jagged horizon, then back at the man who had upended the world. "Because I have spent my life writing about kings who were afraid of their own shadows. You just walked away from God and the Devil without looking back. If anyone can hold onto their name in this place, it is the man who made the world fear it."

Arghun sank to his knees, not in worship, but out of sheer exhaustion. "If you are building a kingdom of nothing, you will need someone to write the laws. Even a ghost needs to know what is forbidden."

Egris looked at the ink-stained hands of the man he had once made a subject through fear. He reached out and gripped the scribe's shoulder, hauling him to his feet.

"I am not building a kingdom of laws," Egris said, staring into the mist. "I am building a nation of the chosen. Walk, Arghun. Your ink is dry, but your mind is still sharp. We will see what the Gray can take from us."

"And if it takes everything?" Arghun whispered.

"Then we will find something new to be."
