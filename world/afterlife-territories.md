# World — The Afterlife Territories

## Overview

The afterlife is divided into three major territories. Each reflects the nature of the power that controls it.

---

## 1. Heaven

**Controlled by:** God  
**Nature:** Order, law, permanence

A beautiful, perfectly ordered empire where every soul has a designated place. Heaven is not cruel — but it is absolute. Every soul who enters is assigned a role based on their life's deeds, their faith, and God's judgment. There is no negotiation.

Heaven is visually stunning and structurally suffocating. Souls who do not fit their assigned roles simply stop questioning after long enough. Whether this is peace or erasure is left to the reader.

**Architecture:** Vast, white, geometric. Cathedrals that have no ceiling. Light that has no source. Everything is clean and proportional and permanent.

**Key location:** The Throne Hall — where God receives souls and delivers judgment. Egris will eventually stand here.

---

## 2. Hell

**Controlled by:** Satan  
**Nature:** Conflict, desire, freedom without structure

A vast, constantly changing battlefield where souls fight for power and freedom. Hell is not a prison in the traditional sense — souls can leave its borders if they are strong enough. But most are not, because Hell rewards power above everything else, and leaving means giving up whatever position you have earned.

Hell is loud, violent, and alive in a way Heaven is not. It is also completely without loyalty. Every alliance is temporary. Every throne is contested.

**Architecture:** Shifting. Volcanic plains, ruined cities, endless fortresses being built and torn down. Nothing stays the same for more than a generation of souls.

**Key location:** The Pit of First Rebellion — the place where Satan first refused God. He has built his throne here as a monument to that moment.

---

## 3. The Wastes

**Controlled by:** No one  
**Nature:** Forgotten, neutral, unclaimed

The neutral land between Heaven and Hell. Filled with forgotten souls, dead civilizations, monsters, and abandoned gods — beings who were once worshipped and lost all their believers, leaving them stranded between realms.

This is where Egris begins. It is the only territory neither God nor Satan fully controls, because neither of them wants it badly enough to hold it.

**Who lives here:**
- Souls whose deaths were never properly recorded — people history forgot
- Ancient warriors from civilizations that no longer exist
- Fallen kings who refused to kneel even after death
- Murdered peasants who died in anonymity
- Soldiers from every era, every culture, every war
- Criminals, outcasts, and people who did not fit any afterlife's judgment
- The Hollow Men — souls erased from history and transformed into predators

**Why it matters:**  
Egris sees these forgotten souls as the perfect foundation for a new nation. They have nothing — no god, no master, no identity anymore. But they also have no chains.

**Architecture:** Ruins of every civilization that ever existed, layered on top of each other. A Roman aqueduct crumbling next to a Mesopotamian ziggurat. A medieval siege wall half-buried in desert sand. Everything forgotten, everything preserved.

---

## The Border Gates

Where all three territories meet, there is a plain of black stone. Two enormous gates stand here:

- **The Gate of Heaven** — White, ornate, permanent
- **The Gate of Hell** — Black, jagged, always slightly open

Every soul who dies arrives on this plain first. God and Satan both have agents here, competing to claim incoming souls before they can choose for themselves.

Egris's first act is driving a crown into the ground between the two gates — claiming the space between them as his own territory.
