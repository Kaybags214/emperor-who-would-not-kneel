# Character — Arghun

> Added during this consistency pass — Arghun recurs across Chapters 3, 4, 9, 10, and 12 with no character sheet anywhere. Pulled directly from the text, not invented.

## Role
Egris's first follower. A scribe, not a soldier.

## Background
Spent twenty-two years in Samarkand recording Egris's tax decrees and execution lists in life — a subject of his empire, not an ally. Follows him into the Wastes anyway, reasoning that if anyone can hold onto their name in a place that erodes memory, it's the man who made the world fear it.

## Why He Follows
Not loyalty. Not worship. Curiosity and a scribe's instinct: *"If you are building a kingdom of nothing, you will need someone to write the laws. Even a ghost needs to know what is forbidden."* He becomes a witness who chooses to stay, recording what happens rather than fighting for it.

## Function in the Story
The camp's record-keeper and its quiet conscience — he's usually the one who asks the plain question everyone else is too afraid to ask ("What are they?" "How did that defeat them?"). By Chapter 9 he's counting the camp's population (forty-three) and still writing everything down.

## Key Relationship
**Egris:** The quiet irony neither of them says aloud — the first person to freely choose Egris is someone he once ruled by fear. Egris trusts him without ever quite acknowledging why.

## Visual Notes (inferred, not yet specified anywhere)
Ink-stained fingers, cracked spectacles, tattered court silk, a heavy satchel of salvaged writing materials he never lets go of, even mid-crisis.
