# Character — God

## Role
Antagonist / Offering. The supreme force of order in the afterlife.

## What God Represents
Order, law, permanence, and obedience. God believes freedom without structure becomes chaos, and that humanity needs a higher authority to survive itself. He is not evil — he is convinced he is the only thing standing between existence and collapse.

## Portrayal
God should not be portrayed as purely good. He has watched civilizations burn because they refused guidance. He has made hard decisions. He does not apologize for them.

He admires Egris's discipline. He considers Egris's independence dangerous not because it threatens God's power — but because God genuinely believes that a being with Egris's influence, operating without divine law, will eventually destroy the people who follow him.

## How He Sees Egris
A flawed instrument. Someone who could accomplish great things under divine authority. Someone who is wasting himself by refusing the one structure that could make his conquests permanent.

## His Offer
A place among the heavenly hosts — not as a servant, but as a general. An eternal empire where every conquered people finally live under one law.

## His Flaw
He cannot conceive of voluntary loyalty that does not eventually require enforcement. He has never met a ruler who led without coercion, because in his experience, freedom without law always collapses.

## Power Type
**Faith** — God's power comes from the worship of billions of souls across history. It is vast, ancient, and structural. He does not need to fight — his power reinforces itself through every soul that believes in him.

## Visual Notes
- Not a bearded man in robes. Something more architectural — light formed into a presence, order made visible.
- Should feel vast and calm, not warm. Beautiful in the way a perfect building is beautiful — impressive, cold, permanent.
- When he is angry, it should feel like a law being violated, not a person being offended.
