# Character — The Former Rival

> Added during this consistency pass — the arc's primary recurring antagonist (Chapters 6–12) had no character sheet anywhere, and no proper name — he is credited only as "the Former Rival" throughout, which appears to be a deliberate choice, not an oversight. Pulled directly from the text, not invented.

## Role
A commander from Egris's living empire — once trusted, then a betrayer, then betrayed in turn. Satan's chosen agent in Arc 1, and the architect of every attack on Egris's camp from Chapter 7 onward (the summoned army, the colossus).

## Visual Identity
Shaved head, single thin braid, a scar crossing his brow and the bridge of his nose. Repaired lamellar armor — cracked plates, re-laced belts. A divided commander's cloak that splits and lifts like a torn banner. Fights with a glaive, which he plants in the ground the way Egris plants his spear — a deliberate visual rhyme between the two as equals, not hero and villain.

## What He Represents
The argument for structure over freedom, made by someone with real standing to make it: *"Refusal makes a hole. And holes fill... Structure is the only mercy the weak ever actually receive."* Unlike Satan or the God/Satan offers in Ch.1–2, his case isn't abstract — he's watched real chaos in the Wastes and believes Egris's principle will get people killed.

## Personality
Level, controlled, capable of old grief breaking through the calm. He is not cruel for its own sake — he counts the dead and "would prefer fewer of them." His hatred for Egris and his genuine belief in what he's arguing are tangled together and neither cancels the other out.

## History with Egris
"I buried men because of him. He buried more because of me." They fought on opposite sides of three campaigns in life; Egris turned a valley of starving refugees into an army that broke the Rival's front line in a single night. The specific betrayal between them is referenced but not yet detailed on the page — an open thread for Arc 2.

## The Gut-Punch Line (Ch.7)
*"You always thought your restraint made you clean. It didn't. It just made the cost land on other people instead of on you."* Egris has no answer for this — it's the one accusation in the arc he can't honestly refute.

## Arc Across Ch.7–12
Argues → is rebuffed → leaves disappointed rather than defeated (Ch.8) → escalates to sending an army of duty-bound echo-warriors (Ch.9–10) → escalates again to a colossus built from the Wastes itself, which collapses when it can't process people choosing to stand without being commanded (Ch.11) → by Ch.12 is confirmed to be gathering a real, living army for a direct confrontation in Arc 2.

## Key Relationship
**Egris:** The philosophical mirror of the whole arc — not God, not Satan, but the man who has walked the version of Egris's road that ends in ruling rather than refusing, and can't forgive Egris for choosing differently the second time.
