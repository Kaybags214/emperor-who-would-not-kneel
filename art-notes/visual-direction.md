# Art Notes — Visual Direction

## Overall Tone

Dark. Heavy. Epic in scale but intimate in character moments. Think less "shonen battle manga" and more "seinen philosophical action" — closer to Berserk or Vagabond in how it balances spectacle with quiet.

---

## Character Design Principles

### Genghis Khan
- Mongol features. Not westernized.
- Practical, worn clothing and armor. This is a man who spent 60 years in the field. Nothing ornamental.
- No crown until the series earns it — and when it appears, it should be meaningful.
- The Reaper's mark: a black vein running from his palm up his wrist. Not glowing. Not magical-looking. Just wrong. Like something leaking through.
- Should look weathered, not old. Authority in his posture, not his expression.

### God
- Not a bearded man in robes.
- Light arranged into form — architectural, geometric, vast. Think the suggestion of a figure, not a literal person.
- Should feel cold, not warm. Beautiful the way a cathedral is beautiful — impressive, permanent, inhuman.
- When angry: does not raise voice. The world around him changes. Laws bending.

### Satan
- More human-looking than God. This is intentional and dangerous.
- Sharp features. A small, genuine-seeming smile. He looks like someone who has been wronged and is completely reasonable about it.
- No obvious evil tells early in the series. The reader should like him before they fully understand him.
- Moves closer to eye level than God. He sits when others stand. He is comfortable.

### The Reaper
- Not a skeleton in a robe.
- A figure that absorbs light — not dark, but an absence. Where light hits him, it stops.
- Calm at all times. Never urgent. He has infinite time.
- Scale is unclear — he should never have a fixed size. Sometimes he seems man-height, sometimes he fills a panel without explanation.
- When he appears, the panel should feel colder. Sparse linework around him. Less detail, not more.

## World Design

### The Border Plain
- Black stone. Flat. Endless. Featureless except for the two gates and the thousands of souls.
- The sky: neither day nor night. Gray. Lightless. No horizon line — the plain seems to go on forever.
- Two gates: one white and ornate (Heaven), one black and jagged (Hell). The white gate has light bleeding through the seams. The black gate is always slightly open.

### Heaven
- Vast, white, geometric. Cathedrals with no ceilings. Light with no source.
- Everything clean and proportional. Overwhelming in its perfection.
- The longer you look at it, the more unsettling it becomes. It is too perfect. Nothing is out of place because nothing is allowed to be.

### Hell
- Shifting, volcanic, constantly under construction and destruction. Loud. Alive.
- Fortresses being built while other fortresses burn. Armies fighting in the streets of cities that change layout between pages.
- The one constant: the Pit of First Rebellion — Satan's throne, built on the site where he first refused God. Old stone. Unchanged. His monument to himself.

### The Wastes
- Ruins of every civilization layered on top of each other. Roman next to Mesopotamian next to pre-colonial. The sediment of forgotten history.
- Gray sky. Dust. Wind that sounds like voices.
- The Hollow Men: visually, these are souls whose features have been partially erased. Not monsters — formerly people. The horror is in what is missing, not what is present.

## Panel Layout Philosophy

- Wide establishing shots for the afterlife territories — emphasize scale.
- Tight close-ups for character moments — Khan's eyes carry half the chapter.
- When two divine powers appear together: asymmetrical panel layout. They do not share equal space. Let their composition fight.
- When Khan is between them: he gets the center panel. Always. His placement is never accidental.
- Silence pages: at least one full page per chapter with no dialogue. Let the art breathe.
- The Reaper: when he appears in a panel, other figures in the same panel should instinctively have less detail. As if his presence draws focus out of everything around him.

---

## Key Visual Moments to Protect

1. **The crown driven into the stone** — Chapter 1. The defining image of the series. Return to it throughout Arc 1.
2. **Khan walking away from both gates** — Chapter 1. Never frame this from the front. Always from behind or from the side. We see where he is going, not his expression.
3. **The first follower standing up** — Chapter 1. One soul stopping kneeling among thousands. Frame it wide so the contrast is visible.
4. **The Reaper's mark appearing** — Chapter 7. No drama. Khan just looks at his hand. The quieter this moment is, the more threatening it feels.
5. **The Wastes, first view** — Chapter 4. Full page or double spread. Establish the scale of what Khan has claimed as his territory.
